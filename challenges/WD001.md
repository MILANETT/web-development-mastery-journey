# WD001 — The Web, Websites and Their Moving Parts

**Deadline:** Sunday, 8 November 2026 at 8:00 PM (EAT, UTC+3)  
**Phase:** Phase 0 — Understanding the Web  
**Type:** Regular challenge

## Objective

Build a clear mental model of what the World Wide Web is, how it relates to the Internet, and what happens at a high level when someone visits a website. You should also be able to distinguish the main roles involved in building a website.

## Concepts covered

- Introduction to the World Wide Web.
- Internet vs World Wide Web.
- How websites work (high-level overview).
- Frontend vs backend development.
- Static vs dynamic websites.

## The challenge

A small business owner wants a website but has almost no technical knowledge. During your first meeting, they ask:

> “Is the Internet the same thing as the Web? And when someone opens my website, what is actually happening? Does every website work the same way?”

Prepare a short, clear technical briefing that answers their questions and demonstrates that you understand the foundations.

## Requirements

1. **Explain the Internet and the Web**
   - Define each in your own words.
   - Explain how they are related.
   - Give at least two examples of things people use through the Internet that are not the World Wide Web.

2. **Explain what a website is and how it works**
   - Describe, in the correct order, the high-level journey from a person entering a website address to seeing a webpage.
   - Identify the main participants in that journey.
   - Explain the difference between a website and a webpage.

3. **Distinguish frontend and backend development**
   - Explain the responsibility of each.
   - Give at least three practical examples of frontend responsibilities and three backend responsibilities.
   - Describe how the two sides work together in a familiar website, such as an online shop.

4. **Compare static and dynamic websites**
   - Explain what makes a website static or dynamic.
   - Give two realistic examples of each.
   - Explain why a business might choose one approach over the other.

5. **Apply your understanding**
   - Choose one familiar website.
   - Briefly identify what a visitor can see and interact with, what might happen behind the scenes, and whether the experience is mostly static or dynamic. Explain your reasoning; do not guess at the site's actual internal technology.

## Constraints

- Use your own explanations. Do not copy definitions from websites or tutorials.
- You may consult learning materials and documentation, but your final explanation must show your own understanding.
- Do not write code for this challenge.
- Do not go deeply into HTTP methods, DNS resolution, browser engines, hosting infrastructure, or the detailed request-response lifecycle. Those belong to upcoming challenges.
- Keep the briefing understandable to a non-technical business owner without making it technically inaccurate.
